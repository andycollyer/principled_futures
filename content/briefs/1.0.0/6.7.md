# Supply-chain security

**What it measures.** Whether the organisation knows what its AI depends on (the supplier, the underlying model, the interfaces, the data flows) and has assessed the security of each, before adoption and after.

**Why it matters.** A deployed AI tool is usually a chain of other companies' products. When one link is compromised or quietly changed, the harm lands on the deployer's customers and staff: their data exposed, decisions about them altered. They did not choose the chain; the organisation that did answers for it.

**The UK position.** Under the UK GDPR a controller may use only processors that give sufficient guarantees of security. The National Cyber Security Centre's twelve supply chain principles start here: "Until you have a clear picture of your supply chain, it will be very hard to establish any meaningful control over it." The voluntary AI security code tells operators to re-run evaluations on released models they intend to use, and to tell users before a model is updated. A Bill to reform network and information systems law was introduced in November 2025; no Act was found on the date checked.

**What good looks like.** A dependency map for each system; a security assessment before adoption; reassessment when the supplier or model changes; supplier incidents tracked; contracts giving audit rights, incident notice and a way out.

**Reading the answer.** A certified vendor covers one link. Ask what the vendor itself depends on, and who would be told if the model changed.

**A defensible figure.** In the government's 2025/2026 breaches survey, 15% of UK businesses had reviewed the risks posed by their immediate suppliers, and 6% those of their wider supply chain.
