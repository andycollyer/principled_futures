# Augmentation-first strategy

**What it measures.** Whether the default, when AI changes a job, is to redesign the work around people, and whether success is measured by output and quality instead of posts removed.

**Why it matters.** The choice decides who gains. One economist puts it this way: "when AI is focused on augmenting humans rather than mimicking them, then humans retain the power to insist on a share of the value created". Where the aim is replacement, workers lose income and bargaining power, and the organisation loses the judgement it needs to check the machine. Field evidence from customer support found that AI used to assist raised productivity, most of all for the least experienced.

**The UK position.** No UK law requires augmentation. Directors must have regard to the interests of employees and to long-term consequences when promoting the company's success. Where collective redundancies are proposed, the employer must consult representatives on ways of avoiding the dismissals and reducing their number, with a view to reaching agreement. Official statistics show that most businesses using AI report no change in headcount so far.

**What good looks like.** A written default of redesign; any departure justified case by case; managers' targets set on throughput and quality; results reported to the board like any other strategy.

**Reading the answer.** Listen for the double message: augmentation in the staff briefing, headcount savings in the business case. Ask which measure managers are rewarded on.

**A defensible figure.** In the Office for National Statistics survey published in July 2026, just under 7% of medium-sized businesses using AI reported that it had reduced their headcount, the highest of any size band; most businesses reported no change.
