## 7.4 Workforce impact assessment (2 October 2026)

Method: content/research/METHOD.md including "Lessons from Domain 3" and "Citations for the source directory".
Every factual claim in the legacy briefing (src/lib/articles.ts, id 7.4) was listed and checked against source
text. Pages were downloaded with curl and the tags stripped (PDFs read with pypdf from the file itself); the
quotation, the figure and all ten reading-list quotes were confirmed by exact-string match in that raw text, and
again with scripts/lead_check.py 7 (read-only, no --add). No summarising reader was relied on for any quotation
or number (web search was used only to find addresses). No NotebookLM notebook.
content/sources/anchors.json was read first; nothing listed there is reused, and no quotation or figure is shared
between 7.1, 7.2, 7.3 and 7.4.

The same figure in the CIPD press release (10 November 2025): "One in six (17%) employers* expect AI to shrink
their workforce over the next year", with the footnote "This excludes employers who don't know if AI tools are
used by employees in their organisation (6% of the total sample)" and "Total sample size was 2,019 senior HR
professionals and decision-makers in the UK. Fieldwork was undertaken between 19 September 2025 and 14 October
2025." (https://www.cipd.org/en/about/press-releases/one-in-six-employers-say-ai-shrink-headcount-autumn-labour-market-outlook/).
The brief says "survey of 2,019 UK employers, 17% expected"; strictly the 17% is of those who knew whether AI
tools were in use. The lead may wish to add "of those able to say".

### Claims in the existing briefing
| # | Claim | Finding |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | "Section 172 requires the board to have regard to employee interests in promoting the company's success" | Supported (s.172(1)(b)). The duty is each director's, owed to the company, and employees' interests are one of six listed matters |
| 2 | "a duty the courts test procedurally, through what the minutes show was actually weighed" | Not found. No case law was read. The section's test is what the director "considers, in good faith" |
| 3 | Extract: "The s172 duty is procedural: stakeholder impact considered, and minuted" | Wrong as stated. s.172 does not require a minute. s.248 requires minutes of directors' meetings without prescribing content; s.414CZA requires larger companies to report how regard was had |
| 4 | "the deployer regime adds its own expectation for workplace AI: workers informed before the system goes into service" | Partly. EU AI Act Art. 26(7): high-risk systems only, deployers who are employers; EU law, not UK; on the Commission's text Chapter III Section 3 applies to Annex III systems from 2 December 2027 |
| 5 | That duty "presumes the organisation has itself understood the impact" | Not found. Art. 26(7) requires telling workers they will be subject to the system, not an impact assessment |
| 6 | "Both point to the same instrument: a workforce impact assessment, performed before adoption" | Not found. No UK or EU source read requires one by that name. Nearest duties: DPIA before high-risk processing (UK GDPR Art. 35), collective redundancy consultation (TULRCA s.188), Acas advice to consult |
| 7 | Assessment content: roles touched, tasks removed and added, redeployment, retraining, reduction, costs, alternatives | Not found as a requirement. Consistent with ILO (job transformation, social dialogue) and Acas; the list is the framework's own |

| 9 | "protection that cannot be manufactured retrospectively, since minute books, like DPIAs, are read by their dates" | Partly. Art. 35(1) requires the DPIA before processing; minutes are dated records. "Protection" overstated |
