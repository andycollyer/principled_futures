# Lawful basis

**What it measures.** Whether the organisation can name a valid legal ground for each use of personal data in its AI: training, the data fed in, and what the system says about people.

**Why it matters.** People whose records or images are absorbed into a model seldom know, so cannot object. Processing without a valid ground is unlawful, and the harm falls on people who never had a say.

**The UK position.** UK GDPR Article 6 lists the lawful bases; at least one must apply. The Information Commission (ICO), in guidance under review, says: "You must break down and separate each distinct processing operation, and identify the purpose and an appropriate lawful basis for each one". Legitimate interests requires a three-part test of purpose, necessity and balance. For training generative models on web-scraped data the regulator regards it as the only basis available, and hard to meet. Since 5 February 2026 a further basis, recognised legitimate interests, covers listed purposes such as crime prevention and emergencies; it may not support a significant decision taken solely by automated processing. Reuse that is compatible still needs its own basis.

**What good looks like.** A record per purpose showing the basis chosen before processing began, the balancing assessment where one is needed, and a fresh review whenever the purpose changes.

**Reading the answer.** Legitimate interests claimed for everything, with no assessment on file, is the usual weak answer; the regulator says it cannot be a default. A basis chosen after launch is another.

**A defensible figure.** In May 2022 the regulator fined a facial recognition company £7,552,800 for, among other failings, scraping people's images with no lawful basis. At its last update, in December 2025, the case was still before the courts.
