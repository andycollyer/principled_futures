# 1.3 Policy architecture (researcher log, 2 October 2026)

Method: content/research/METHOD.md, including "Lessons from Domain 3" and "Citations for the source directory".
Every web source was fetched with curl and read as raw page text with the tags stripped; PDFs were read as text
extracted from the PDF itself. A search tool was used only to find two URLs (the FRC annual review and the DSIT
adoption research); both documents were then read at source. The quotation, the figure and every reading-list quote
were matched as exact strings in the raw text after normalising white space and quote marks.

Angle. The documents: whether a use policy, an acceptable-use policy and an escalation policy exist, are approved and
are current. Who owns AI decisions and whether the board has a remit are separate briefs.

## Not verified, and cautions

- The FRC figure counts companies that reported a policy in their annual report. Companies may have one and not
  report it, so it is a floor, and the brief says "reported".
- The DSIT finding is qualitative (100 interviews); the brief gives no percentage. The quantitative survey was not
  searched for a policy question in full; none was seen.
- AIME is the consultation version. The government response was seen on the consultation page but the final tool was
  not found or read; the brief calls it a draft.
- "Board-approved" in "What good looks like" follows the criterion's own level three. Outside banking it is good
  practice, not an expectation of any regulator read here.
- The brief's "an older policy is out of date" is an inference from the commencement date, not a quotation.
- The top level of the criterion ("independently assessed; a model others copy") has no source.
- ISO/IEC 42001, which has a clause on AI policy, could not be read (HTTP 403) and is not cited.
- Body word count 287 including the six bold labels. Reading list: 10 items.
