# Independent assurance

**What it measures.** Whether anyone outside the team that runs AI governance checks that it works: a review that is scoped, scheduled, carried out by someone free to disagree, and followed through.

**Why it matters.** People affected by AI decisions cannot inspect the controls behind them. They rely on someone independent having looked. Government research with the public found: "Participants felt that, if assurance were delivered only by an AI developer that is profit motivated, it would not be trustworthy." Self-review tells a board what the organisation believes, not whether it is right.

**The UK position.** No general law requires an independent audit of AI governance. The Corporate Governance Code expects a listed company's audit committee to review internal audit and, where there is none, to explain how internal assurance is achieved. The Information Commission (ICO) can require an organisation to permit an assessment of its data protection compliance. Government's 2025 review, though, found the quality of AI assurance services unclear and the standards still developing.

**What good looks like.** Review scoped by risk, starting with systems that decide about people; a reviewer independent of those who built or run the system; findings given an owner and a date, and tracked to closure; results reported to the board. Internal audit standards require an external assessment of the audit function itself at least once every five years.

**Reading the answer.** A certificate is evidence only if someone can say what it covered. The internal auditors' own AI framework warns that internal audit is likely to give only limited assurance on AI.

**A defensible figure.** In November 2024 the government estimated that 524 firms supplied AI assurance goods and services in the UK, 84 of them specialised.
