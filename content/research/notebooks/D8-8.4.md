## Not verified, and cautions

- Can a UK supplier rely on the right? The definition of protected undertaking has no geographic limit, and the duty
  sits on the EU reporting customer. But the right exists through each member state's transposing law, and how it
  applies to a supplier outside the EU, or under a contract governed by English law, was not researched and is a
  legal question. The brief says only that the right is EU law and that no UK equivalent was found.
- "Member states must transpose the directive by 19 March 2027": Art. 5(1) sets that date for Arts 1 to 3; Art. 4
  (due diligence) has a later date, 26 July 2028. The brief's sentence is about the reporting provisions.
- "for financial years from 1 January 2027": this is the application date of Art. 3 of Regulation (EU) 2026/1560
  (the Annex II cap). The directive's own right depends on national transposition. Until then the position in any
  one member state is not known.
- A drafting difference: the adopted text C(2026) 5011 Art. 3(1) says the standard establishes the cap "for
  undertakings not subject to mandatory reporting requirements"; the Official Journal text says "for undertakings
  subject to". The Official Journal text is followed.
- Annex II also lists some Comprehensive Module datapoints (C1, C5, C6, C7) and leaves out some Basic Module ones
  (B2, B4, B5, fatalities under B9, the pay gap under B10, B11). So "Basic Module = the limit" is wrong in both
  directions. The brief avoids the equation.
- Undertakings with more than 1 000 employees but turnover at or below EUR 450 million are neither reporting nor
  protected (SWD p.5 printed). Not in the brief for length.
- "data produced under pressure is often guessed" and the harm to "the smaller firm's staff" are reasoning, not
  sourced findings.
- "What good looks like" (written position, offer of the standard data, log) follows the criterion's level text;
  no source prescribes it.
