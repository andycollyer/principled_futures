# 8.6 Sustainability data quality (researcher log, 2 October 2026)

Method: content/research/METHOD.md, including "Lessons from Domain 3" and "Citations for the source directory".
Every factual claim in the existing briefing (src/lib/articles.ts, id 8.6) was listed and checked against the
primary source text. Every web source below was fetched with curl and read as raw page text with the tags
stripped; PDFs were read as text extracted from the PDF itself (pypdf). No summarising tool was used for any
quotation or figure. The quotation, the figure and all nine reading-list quotes were matched as exact strings in
that raw text (after normalising white space and quote marks).

Angle. The legacy briefing is almost entirely advice with no legal content. This brief anchors the advice in what
UK rules and the UK regulator already ask of larger companies about method, traceability and assurance, and says
plainly that none of it binds a small company.

Other sentences in the brief that rest on a specific passage (paraphrased, not quoted):
- "directors' reports must state the methodologies used": S.I. 2008/410 Sch. 7 para. 20F: "The directors’ report
  must state the methodologies used to calculate the information disclosed under paragraph 20D(1), (2) and (3)."
- "use verifiable data and show how estimates were made": Environmental Reporting Guidelines, PDF page 46:
  "organisations must use verifiable data where reasonably practicable" and "must use a reasonable estimate ... and
  show how estimates were made."
- "verifiable when the inputs behind it can be corroborated": UK SRS S1 para. D21: "Information is verifiable if it
  is possible to corroborate either the information itself or the inputs used to derive it."
- "a second person checking figures": GHG Protocol Corporate Standard, Chapter 7, Table 4: "Check a sample of input
  data for transcription errors". The "second person" is my wording.
- "over some or all of the metrics assured": the source reads "over some, or all, of the metrics being assured".

## Not verified, and cautions

- "No law tells a small company how to keep sustainability data" rests on the rules read and on finding nothing
  else; a full search of UK legislation was not done.
- The size tests in S.I. 2008/410 para. 20B(2) are recorded as shown on legislation.gov.uk today. Company size
  thresholds were changed in 2025; whether this paragraph on the site is fully up to date was not checked. The
  brief states no threshold.
- The quotation is addressed to listed companies reporting climate metrics, not to SMEs. The brief says "In a 2023
  review of climate metrics".
- The figure is about FTSE 350 companies. It is used to show what assurance means at the top of the scale, not to
  describe SMEs.
- UK SRS S1: the brief says "published in February 2026". It does not say who must apply it; S1 para. 69 leaves
  that to others, and the FCA's and government's decisions on that were not researched in this pass.
- The sentence about AI tools filling gaps is reasoning without a source.
- The criterion's level-four wording ("tracked via telemetry") has no counterpart in any source read.
- Body word count: 287 words including the six bold labels. Reading list: 9 items.
