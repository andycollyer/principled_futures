# Basic sustainability data

**What it measures.** Whether the organisation can produce the basic data set that the EU's voluntary standard defines: energy, emissions, water, waste, workforce, safety, pay and conduct.

**Why it matters.** Customers and lenders build their own public reports on suppliers' numbers. A firm that reconstructs its figures for each questionnaire gives different answers to different askers, and nobody can tell which is right. The people misled are those who rely on the customer's report. Energy used by AI systems is part of the footprint and is easily left out.

**The UK position.** The Basic Module is EU law, not UK law: Annex I to Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2026/1560, published on 21 September 2026, which supersedes the Commission's 2025 Recommendation of EFRAG's standard. It has eleven disclosures, B1 to B11. The regulation says: "This module is the target approach for micro-undertakings and constitutes a minimum requirement for other undertakings". Several environmental items are voluntary for firms with ten employees or fewer. No UK equivalent for small firms was found: energy and carbon reporting binds quoted and large companies, and government encourages others to report voluntarily.

**What good looks like.** One maintained data set covering B1 to B11, a named owner, the method noted beside each figure, and a yearly refresh.

**Reading the answer.** Energy bills in a folder are fragments. Ask when the set was last refreshed and whether the emissions method is written down. The standard asks for comparatives from the second year.

**A defensible figure.** The Commission's staff estimate the first-year cost of the Basic Module at EUR 3,500 to 4,500 for firms with fewer than 20 employees and EUR 7,500 to 12,800 for firms with 20 to 250 (SWD(2026) 500, printed page 20).
