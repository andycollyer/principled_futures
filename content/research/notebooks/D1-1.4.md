# 1.4 AI inventory and register (researcher log, 2 October 2026)

Method: content/research/METHOD.md, including "Lessons from Domain 3" and "Citations for the source directory".
Every web source was fetched with curl and read as raw page text with the tags stripped; PDFs were read as text
extracted from the PDF itself. A search tool was used only to find two URLs (the FRC annual review and the DSIT
adoption research); both documents were then read at source. The quotation, the figure and every reading-list quote
were matched as exact strings in the raw text after normalising white space and quote marks.

Angle. The register itself: what must or should be listed, by whom, and how it is kept current, including systems
bought in or built into other software. The internal-transparency brief already covers what the organisation knows
about its AI, with UK GDPR Article 30 and the 250-staff figure and the audit-framework quotation. None of those is
used here. This brief rests on the banking model inventory, the government's internal inventory and transparency
records, the voluntary cyber security code and the National Audit Office's survey.

## Not verified, and cautions

- The figure pre-dates the mandatory requirement (February 2024) and comes from a self-reported survey in autumn
  2023. It shows how little a voluntary register was used; it does not measure compliance today. The live repository
  count (152 records on the day of reading) was not used because it changes.
- The criterion's top level ("reused commercially in due diligence") has no source. The brief does not claim it.
- "Features switched on inside software already licensed are easily missed" is the brief's own caution, with no
  source; the lead may prefer to cut it.
- SS1/23 concerns models, which is wider and narrower than AI: it includes non-AI models and may not catch AI tools
  that are not models used for business decisions. The brief says "model inventory".
- The AI cyber security code is addressed to developers, system operators and data custodians; whether an ordinary
  business user of bought-in AI is a "system operator" was not examined.
- AIME is the consultation version; the final tool was not found or read.
- ISO/IEC 42001 could not be read (HTTP 403).
- Body word count 287 including the six bold labels. Reading list: 9 items.
