# 8.5 AI environmental footprint (researcher log, 2 October 2026)

Method: content/research/METHOD.md, including "Lessons from Domain 3" and "Citations for the source directory".
Every factual claim in the existing briefing (src/lib/articles.ts, id 8.5) was listed and checked against the
primary source text. Every web source below was fetched with curl and read as raw page text with the tags
stripped; PDFs were read as text extracted from the PDF itself (pypdf). No summarising tool was used for any
quotation or figure. Web search was used only to find addresses. The quotation, the figures and all ten
reading-list quotes were matched as exact strings in that raw text (after normalising white space and quote marks).

Angle. The other three briefs written in this pass cover the data, the owner and the commercial use. This one is
about the count itself: what a deploying organisation can and cannot know about the footprint of the AI it buys.

Other sentences in the brief that rest on a specific passage (paraphrased, not quoted):
- "Energy and carbon reporting for large companies covers their own energy use; emissions from purchased services
  ... are voluntary": Environmental Reporting Guidelines, Chapter 2, p.39: "You are not required to report on other
  emissions associated with inputs into your company (such as emissions from your supply chain)". S.I. 2008/410
  Sch. 7 para. 20D(1)-(3): combustion of gas, fuel for transport, and "the purchase of electricity by the company
  for its own use".
- "where cloud and AI sit": GHG Protocol Scope 3 Standard, section 5.5, Category 1: "Products include both goods
  (tangible products) and services (intangible products)." The standard does not name cloud or AI; this is my
  reading and should be checked by the lead.
- "Of commercial models": the sentence before the quotation reads "There is also a lack of transparency regarding
  the size and implementation of most user-facing commercial AI models, which makes it impossible to measure their
  compute requirements and ensuing energy demands."
- "document energy consumption, but for regulators on request, not for customers": AI Act Article 53(1)(a)
  ("for the purpose of providing it, upon request, to the AI Office and the national competent authorities") and
  Annex XI Section 1 point 2(e) ("known or estimated energy consumption of the model"). Annex XII (information for
  downstream providers) was fetched and contains no occurrence of the word "energy".
- "communities whose power and drinking water a data centre draws on": NEPC web page: "These rapidly growing
  resource demands could have far-reaching effects, such as creating competition for renewable energy or drinking
  water."
- "a smaller, task-specific model": Luccioni et al., abstract.

## Not verified, and cautions

- The figure is for all data centres worldwide, not AI and not the UK. The brief says so. It is a projection in the
  agency's Base Case; the same report gives 670 to 1,260 TWh for 2030 across its cases.
- The brief says "No UK law requires a business to report the footprint of the AI it buys." This rests on the
  reporting rules read (S.I. 2008/410 Sch. 7 Part 7A; the 2019 guidelines) and on finding nothing else. A full
  search of UK legislation was not done. UK SRS S2, now available for voluntary use, asks for Scope 3; whether and
  when it becomes mandatory for anyone was not researched here.
- "where cloud and AI sit" is my reading of Category 1; the GHG Protocol text read does not mention cloud or AI.
- EU AI Act: only the Commission's copy was read. It describes itself as the 13 June 2024 version while listing an
  Article 4a, so it is partly updated. The brief states no date and no penalty.
- The quotation's page: the PDF's page 44 carries the printed number 44.
- The engineering report is by the National Engineering Policy Centre (Royal Academy of Engineering with the IET
  and BCS). It and the two government pages are in the reading list but are not named in the brief, for length.
- "Water" in "What it measures": no method for a deployer to estimate water use was read. The criterion text says
  "energy and resource cost"; the brief keeps water because the engineering report treats it as a main impact.
- Body word count: 284 words including the six bold labels. Reading list: 10 items.
