# Incident response integration

**What it measures.** Whether a failing AI system triggers the organisation's existing incident response: named scenarios, known reporting duties, rehearsal, and lessons fed back.

**Why it matters.** A model can give wrong or unfair answers for weeks with no alarm. If nobody treats that as an incident, the people refused, overcharged or exposed are not told and not put right, and the same fault harms the next person.

**The UK position.** No UK law sets a general duty to report AI failures. Where personal data is involved, a breach must be notified to the Information Commission (ICO) within 72 hours where feasible, and to the individuals when the risk to them is high. The government's voluntary code for AI security says "Developers and System Operators shall create, test and maintain an AI system incident management plan and an AI system recovery plan." Its governance code asks boards to see the plan exercised at least annually. Under the EU AI Act the reporting duty is the provider's: a serious incident involving a high-risk system goes to the market surveillance authority within 15 days of awareness, 10 after a death, two for a widespread infringement or serious disruption of critical infrastructure. Deployers tell the provider first. The Commission's consolidated copy, as at 27 July 2026, shows that article unamended; it gives a general application date of 2 August 2026 and no separate date for this duty, while the high-risk requirements themselves apply later.

**What good looks like.** AI failure types written into the plan; reporting duties mapped beforehand; logs preserved; an AI scenario exercised yearly; each incident reviewed and the lessons applied.

**Reading the answer.** The usual weak answer is that it would be handled like any outage. Ask when an AI scenario was last rehearsed, and what changed afterwards.

**A defensible figure.** The government's 2025/2026 breaches survey found that 25% of UK businesses had a formal incident response plan, and 45% had none of the response measures it asked about. It covers cyber incidents generally, not AI.
