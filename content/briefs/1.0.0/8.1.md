# Value-chain position

**What it measures.** Whether the organisation knows which customers, lenders and public buyers must report on sustainability, which duties reach it as a supplier, and what each may ask.

**Why it matters.** A reporting duty placed on a large company travels down to its suppliers as questionnaires. A firm caught by surprise answers with guesses, and guessed figures pass into reports that investors, lenders and the public rely on. That includes the energy its AI tools use. The EU legislator recorded: "There is evidence that undertakings in the value chain, including small and medium-sized enterprises, receive disproportionate requests for information from reporting undertakings".

**The UK position.** No UK law was found that limits what a customer may ask. An EU customer must report only if it exceeds both EUR 450 million net turnover and 1,000 employees (Directive (EU) 2026/470). Financial Conduct Authority rules require listed companies to report against UK Sustainability Reporting Standards for periods from 1 January 2027, with value-chain emissions on a comply-or-explain basis. Quoted and large companies must report energy and carbon. Central government asks bidders for a Carbon Reduction Plan on contracts above £5 million a year.

**What good looks like.** A written map: each major customer, lender and public buyer; the duty it is under; the data it may legitimately ask for; revised when clients or thresholds change.

**Reading the answer.** Knowing who the big customers are is not knowing their obligations. Ask for the map and its date. One drawn before 2026 is out of date.

**A defensible figure.** The European Commission estimates that the 2026 directive cut the number of companies in scope of mandatory EU sustainability reporting by about 85% (staff working document SWD(2026) 500, printed page 3).
