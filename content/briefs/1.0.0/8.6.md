# Sustainability data quality

**What it measures.** Whether sustainability figures are gathered once, from a known source, with an owner and a written method, and then reused for every customer, lender and report.

**Why it matters.** Figures rebuilt for each request drift apart. Customers, lenders and the public then rely on numbers nobody can trace. The risk grows when an AI tool fills the gaps, because it will produce a plausible number whether or not one exists.

**The UK position.** No law tells a small company how to keep sustainability data. Rules for large companies show what is expected: directors' reports must state the methodologies used to calculate energy and emissions figures, and government guidance says to use verifiable data and show how estimates were made. The UK Sustainability Reporting Standards, published in February 2026, treat information as verifiable when the inputs behind it can be corroborated. In a 2023 review of climate metrics, the Financial Reporting Council said: "We also encourage companies to be transparent about data limitations, including explanations of estimations and areas where it is not feasible to collect data."

**What good looks like.** One dataset; an owner for each line; sources and conversion factors recorded; a refresh date; estimates marked as estimates; a second person checking figures before release.

**Reading the answer.** Ask for last year's electricity figure and where it came from. If two documents give two numbers, the data is being rebuilt each time. Independent assurance sits at the top of the scale; even among the largest companies it is usually limited.

**A defensible figure.** 83% of assurance engagements for FTSE 350 companies in 2023 involved limited assurance over some or all of the metrics assured (Financial Reporting Council market study, February 2025, paragraph 18).
