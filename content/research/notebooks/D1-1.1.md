# 1.1 Board mandate (researcher log, 2 October 2026)

Method: content/research/METHOD.md, including "Lessons from Domain 3" and "Citations for the source directory".
Every web source was fetched with curl and read as raw page text with the tags stripped; PDFs were read as text
extracted from the PDF itself. A search tool was used only to find two URLs (the FRC annual review and the DSIT
adoption research); both documents were then read at source. The quotation, the figure and every reading-list quote
were matched as exact strings in the raw text after normalising white space and quote marks.

Angle. The mandate itself: is AI in the written remit of the board or a committee, is it reported on, is it minuted.
Who is the named owner is left to the roles brief; the policy set and the register have their own briefs.

Paraphrased passages the brief rests on:
- "names new technologies such as AI among the controls a board might treat as material": guidance para 272, "While
  the board decides which controls are material these could include, but are not limited to, controls over: ...
  information and technology risks including cybersecurity, data protection and new technologies (e.g. artificial
  intelligence)."
- "a responsibility delegated to a committee should be written into its terms of reference": guidance para 223,
  "this should be clearly written in the committee’s terms of reference".
- "applies to listed companies on a comply or explain basis": Code, Introduction, "Application" and "Reporting on the
  Code" (commercial companies category or closed-ended investment funds category; Listing Rules).
- "does not mention AI": the extracted text of the Code PDF contains neither "artificial" nor "technolog".
- Wates: "The terms of each committee should be set out including authorities delegated to it. A board retains
  responsibility for any final decisions." (Principle Three, p.16).

## Not verified, and cautions

- Whether any court or regulator has applied section 174 to a failure to oversee AI: not searched beyond the sources above.
- The FRC guidance is edited in place; paragraph numbers may shift. Read on 2 October 2026.
- "Listed companies" in the brief is shorthand for the two Listing Rules categories named in the Code.
- The 86% counts annual reports that mention AI at all. It says nothing about the quality of oversight.
- The criterion's top level ("externally assured and benchmarked") has no source: nothing read requires external
  assurance of AI oversight, and the FRC guidance (para 274) says there is no expectation of external assurance of
  material controls. The brief says "independently reviewed" only as a description of good practice.
- Body word count 280 including the six bold labels. Reading list: 10 items.
