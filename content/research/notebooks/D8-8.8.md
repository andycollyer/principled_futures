# 8.8 Commercial leverage (researcher log, 2 October 2026)

Method: content/research/METHOD.md, including "Lessons from Domain 3" and "Citations for the source directory".
Every factual claim in the existing briefing (src/lib/articles.ts, id 8.8) was listed and checked against the
primary source text. Every web source below was fetched with curl and read as raw page text with the tags

(read-only, no --add): 0 issues.

Angle. The legacy briefing asserts that sustainability readiness wins contracts and lowers the cost of finance.
This brief states what the UK rules actually do (a gate, and a minimum weighting for social value) and what the
one UK regulator to have looked at sustainability-linked lending actually found (small effects, slow uptake among
smaller firms). The evidence does not support the stronger claim, and the brief says so.

Deliberately not used as a figure: the contract value at which PPN 006 applies ("above £5 million per year"). It
was read at source (PPN 006, para. 3), but the 8.1 brief already uses it as a figure, so this brief says "major

minimum 10% weighting") is described as "a minimum weighting": a bare "10%" is already an anchor for another brief.
The lead may prefer to restore either number.

Other sentences in the brief that rest on a specific passage (paraphrased, not quoted):
- "a bidder must supply a Carbon Reduction Plan as a condition of participation": PPN 006 para. 10: in-scope
  organisations "should include, as a condition of participation, a requirement for bidding suppliers to provide a
  CRP". Note "should", and "save where it would not be relevant to the contract or proportionate" (para. 3). The
  brief's "must supply" describes the bidder's position once the buyer has applied the measure.
- "a tender that fails a condition must be disregarded": Procurement Act 2023 s.19(3)(a).
- "pass or fail": PPN 006 guidance on adopting and applying conditions of participation, para. 7: "Questions 1 and
  2 should be assessed on a pass/fail basis based on self-declarations by the supplier."
- "central government must give social value a minimum weighting": PPN 002 para. 10.
- "environmental benefit is one outcome a buyer may choose": PPN 002 Social Value Model, outcome 4a: "Deliver
  additional environmental benefits in the performance of the contract, including working towards net zero
  greenhouse gas emissions".
- "slow to develop for smaller firms": FCA letter p.2 (string above).
- "hold robust evidence for environmental claims": CMA guidance: "They should hold robust, credible, relevant and
  up to date evidence that supports them."

## Not verified, and cautions

- The figure comes from the FCA's conversations with market participants in March and April 2023. It is an
  observation about investment-grade borrowers in a market of large loans, three years old, and the letter itself
  says the regulator does not directly regulate that market. It is used to show how small the price effect has been
  where it has been observed, not as a rate a small firm can expect. No later FCA publication on the subject was
  searched for.
- "On finance the evidence is thin" is my summary of what was and was not found. British Business Bank and UK
  Finance material, which may bear on this, could not be read.
- PPN 006 says buyers "should" include the condition and may disapply it where not relevant or proportionate; the
  brief's "must supply" is the bidder's position where the condition is applied. The lead may prefer "is normally
  required to supply".
- PPN 002 and PPN 006 bind central government bodies only. The brief says "central government" each time.
- The PPNs and the National Procurement Policy Statement were issued under the previous government (the UK Compute
  Roadmap page carries a banner to that effect; the procurement pages do not). All were shown as current on gov.uk
  today, with no withdrawal notice. Worth re-checking at each source review.
- "In public tenders, readiness is more often an entry ticket than a scoring advantage" is my inference from the
  pass or fail rule. It is not a measured finding.
- The CMA guidance dates from 2021; the consumer law behind it was replaced in 2025 and the guidance may have been
  reissued. Not checked. The business-to-business position is described by the guidance as "less comprehensive".
- The sentence about AI-drafted bids is reasoning without a source.
- Private-sector procurement: nothing read. The brief makes no claim about it.
- Body word count: 285 words including the six bold labels. Reading list: 10 items.
