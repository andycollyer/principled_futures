# Commercial leverage

**What it measures.** Whether the organisation uses its sustainability evidence on purpose, in bids and in finance conversations, and records whether it made a difference.

**Why it matters.** Used honestly, good evidence earns work. Used loosely, it misleads the buyer or lender and takes work from an honest rival. AI-drafted bids sharpen the risk: they assert credentials fluently whether or not anyone has checked.

**The UK position.** For major central government contracts, a bidder must supply a Carbon Reduction Plan as a condition of participation, and a tender that fails a condition must be disregarded. The plan is pass or fail: guidance says plans should not be scored or compared. Separately, central government must give social value a minimum weighting; environmental benefit is one outcome a buyer may choose. On finance the evidence is thin. Reviewing sustainability-linked loans, the Financial Conduct Authority wrote: "Small savings on margins may be outweighed by costs and negotiation time with lenders or legal advisors." It found the market slow to develop for smaller firms. The Competition and Markets Authority expects businesses to hold robust evidence for environmental claims.

**What good looks like.** A maintained set of bid answers drawn from one dataset; the same figures given to customers and lenders; a note after each tender and loan of whether sustainability counted.

**Reading the answer.** Ask for one contract or loan where it mattered, and how they know. In public tenders, readiness is more often an entry ticket than a scoring advantage.

**A defensible figure.** Margin step-ups for missed sustainability targets were around 2.5 basis points, capped at about 5, among investment-grade borrowers (Financial Conduct Authority letter, 29 June 2023). A basis point is one hundredth of one per cent.
