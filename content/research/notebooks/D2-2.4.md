# 2.4 Deployer operational controls (researcher log, 2 October 2026)

Method: content/research/METHOD.md, including "Lessons from Domain 3", "Citations for the source directory" and
the 2 October 2026 update on the Commission's consolidated copy of the EU AI Act. Every web source was fetched with
curl and read as raw page text with the tags stripped; PDFs were read as text extracted from the PDF itself (pypdf).
No summarising tool was used. The quotation, the figures and all ten reading-list quotes were matched as exact
strings in raw text, and `python3 scripts/lead_check.py 2` (read-only, no `--add`) reported 0 issues.

EU AI Act source. European Commission, AI Act Service Desk (ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/...). Every
article page read carries the banner "The text displayed here is based on the EUR-Lex consolidated version of the
AI Act as at 27 July 2026" and marks Digital Omnibus changes NEW or AMENDED. Contradiction recorded: the footer of
the same pages still says "The text used in this tool is the ‘Artificial Intelligence Act (Regulation (EU)
2024/1689), Official version of 13 June 2024’". The banner and the AMENDED markers were relied on (the more precise
passage). All application dates below come from Article 113 on that site, read in this pass.

Sources that blocked (not bypassed, not cited):
- iso.org/standard/65694.html (ISO 31000) and iso.org/standard/77304.html (ISO/IEC 23894): HTTP 403, twice.
- rm.coe.int/1680afae3c and coe.int Framework Convention on AI page: HTTP 403.
- equalityhumanrights.com/guidance/artificial-intelligence-public-services: HTTP 403.
- iod.com AI governance page: HTTP 403.
- gov.uk/government/publications/international-ai-safety-report-2026: HTTP 404 (the report was read on
  internationalaisafetyreport.org instead).
- gov.uk Orange Book "Risk Appetite Guidance Note": HTTP 404 at the guessed address, and the collection page says
  it was deleted on 29 July 2026 "as they were out of date". Not cited.

Angle. The organisation as user of someone else's AI: instructions, input data, monitoring, logs. The only binding
text that lists these four things is Article 26 of the EU AI Act, which is not UK law and is not yet in
application. The brief therefore leads with what UK sources ask (a voluntary government code, ICO guidance, the
PRA on vendor models) and gives the EU rule with its date.

Other sentences resting on a specific passage (paraphrased):
- "organisations that deploy AI": the code defines System Operators as "any type of business or organisation
  across any sector that has responsibility for embedding / deploying an AI model and system within their
  infrastructure".
- "bought-in AI should be reviewed regularly, with the ability to switch provider": ICO, "you should regularly
  review any outsourced services and be able to modify them or switch to another provider if their use is no
  longer compliant in your circumstances".
- "validate and monitor their own use of vendor models": SS1/23 Principle 2.6(b)(iii).
- "use in accordance with the instructions": Art. 26(1). "relevant and sufficiently representative": Art. 26(4),
  "to the extent the deployer exercises control over the input data". "suspension": Art. 26(5).
- "treated as its provider": Art. 25(1)(c).
