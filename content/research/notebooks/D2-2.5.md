# Domain 2, criterion 2.5 Risk treatment: research log

(researcher log, 2 October 2026; not yet re-checked by the lead)

Method: content/research/METHOD.md in full, including "Lessons from Domain 3", "Citations for the source
directory" and the 2 October 2026 update on the Commission's consolidated copy of the EU AI Act. Every factual
claim in the legacy briefing (src/lib/articles.ts, id 2.5) was listed and checked against source text. Pages and
PDFs were downloaded with curl; HTML tags were stripped and PDF text was extracted from the file itself with
pypdf. The quotation, the figure and all ten reading-list quotes were then confirmed by exact-string match using
the project's own fetch and normalisation (scripts/source_check.py: fetch, norm), so they are tested the way the
fortnightly check will test them. Web search was used only to find addresses; nothing is cited from a search
summary. content/sources/anchors.json was read first; nothing listed there is reused, and no quotation or figure
is shared between the four briefs 2.5 to 2.8. EU AI Act: read only from the Commission's AI Act Service Desk
copy, each article page stating "based on the EUR-Lex consolidated version of the AI Act as at 27 July 2026".

### Claims in the legacy briefing
| # | Claim | Finding | Source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Every identified risk carries an explicit decision: mitigate, transfer or accept | partly | Orange Book D6 lists six options including avoiding the risk and taking it to pursue an opportunity; NIST MANAGE 1.3 lists four including avoiding. The legacy list omits avoid (stop the activity). |
| 2 | Accept means a named person with authority signs that residual risk sits inside appetite; dated, owned, recorded | partly | Good practice in Orange Book D6 (compare with risk appetite) and D8 (those accountable for approving; dates). ICO DPIA guidance asks for sign-off. No general legal duty of sign-off was found outside the DPIA. |
| 3 | "Risk identification without treatment is worse than ignorance: it documents that the organisation knew" | not found | Rhetorical. No source read says a register is treated as an admission. |
| 4 | Transfer includes "insurance where the market offers it" | partly | The Lloyd's Market Association says AI is already within the definition of computer system in model cyber clauses and cover depends on the clause. No evidence was read on availability or price of AI-specific cover. |
| 5 | "equality-law exposure for discriminatory decisions generally stays with the deployer regardless of what the contract says about the vendor" | partly | s.109(2) and s.144(1) support the direction. But s.109(2) needs an agent acting with authority, and no case on software suppliers was read; and s.144 does not stop a supplier indemnifying the organisation for the cost. |
| 6 | Mitigation "often means human-process changes" because "the deployer rarely controls the model" | not found | Judgement. EU AI Act Art. 9(5)(c) does treat information and training for deployers as a risk measure. |
| 7 | "an organisation that accepts a documented, appetite-consistent risk is governing; one that accepts by silence is drifting" | partly | Consistent with "retaining the risk by informed decision" (Orange Book D6). The wording is judgement. |
| 8 | At maturity treatment effectiveness is measured and residual risk reported against appetite | supported | Orange Book D8 (key performance measures and control indicators) and D10 (monitoring before, during and after treatment). As practice, not law. |
| 9 | "pick any risk identified more than six months ago and ask what changed" | not found | A test of the author's; no source gives six months. Kept in the brief as a question, not a fact. |

### Proposed corrections (CORRECTIONS.md format; not added to that file)

## 2.5 Treating risk (briefing)
| # | Current wording | Problem | Proposed |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 2.5-a | "mitigate ... transfer ... or accept" (title, extract and body) | Omits the option of stopping the activity, which both the Orange Book and NIST list | "reduce it, share it, stop the activity, or accept it" |
| 2.5-b | "equality-law exposure for discriminatory decisions generally stays with the deployer regardless of what the contract says about the vendor" | Overstates: a supplier can indemnify the cost; agency of a software supplier is untested | "a contract can move the cost to the supplier but cannot exclude the organisation's duties under the Equality Act to the people affected" |
| 2.5-c | "insurance where the market offers it" | Unsourced | "insurance, where the policy's cyber clause affirms cover instead of limiting or excluding it" |
| 2.5-d | "Risk identification without treatment is worse than ignorance: it documents that the organisation knew." and extract "a list of admissions" | Unsourced legal implication | "A risk that is recorded and left untreated shows the organisation knew and did not decide" |
| 2.5-e | "tightening review (3.5)" | Internal question number in client text | "tightening human review" |
| 2.5-f | "transfer (contractual allocation to the vendor ...)" | "Transfer" suggests the risk leaves; the Orange Book says "sharing" | "share (contract terms with the supplier, or insurance)" |

### Could not verify
- Whether any UK court or tribunal has treated an AI supplier as an "agent" under s.109(2). No case was read.
- Whether any insurer offers affirmative AI cover to UK small and medium-sized firms, or applies explicit AI exclusions. Only the market association's general statements were read.
- The assignment's starting point "tolerate, treat, transfer, terminate" is not in the May 2023 Orange Book; the current text uses the six options in D6. Earlier editions were not read.
- The Lloyd's survey page carries no publication date; "2025" is from the text (survey conducted mid-2025).
