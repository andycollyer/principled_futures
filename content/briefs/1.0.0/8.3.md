# Comprehensive readiness

**What it measures.** Whether, where a lender or major client requires it, the organisation can answer the fuller set: strategy, emissions targets, climate risks, human rights policies and incidents, and value-chain emissions.

**Why it matters.** These answers are judgements and estimates, not meter readings. A target nobody can evidence, or a value-chain estimate with no stated method, misleads the lender or client who relies on it, and through them investors and the public. The emissions of bought-in AI services sit in the value chain and are estimates too.

**The UK position.** The Comprehensive Module, C1 to C9, is the second part of the EU voluntary standard in Delegated Regulation (EU) 2026/1560, which states: "Applying the Basic Module is a prerequisite for applying the Comprehensive Module." Value-chain (Scope 3) emissions are not among the nine; the standard treats them as an optional addition, measured across fifteen categories. Financial Conduct Authority rules for listed companies put Scope 3 on a comply-or-explain basis, recognising that many struggle to obtain the data. Those companies will turn to suppliers.

**What good looks like.** Capability built for named counterparties: targets with base year and target year, climate risks assessed, a human rights policy and complaints route, value-chain estimates with the method stated.

**Reading the answer.** Ask who required it and what was sent. Answers rebuilt for each request are not readiness. The standard does not require assurance, so a claim of assured data needs the assurer named.

**A defensible figure.** For firms with 20 to 250 employees, the Commission's staff estimate the cost of both modules together at EUR 17,500 to 22,800 in the first year and EUR 8,500 to 11,000 a year after (SWD(2026) 500, printed page 20).
