# Ongoing monitoring

**What it measures.** Whether the risk of each AI system is looked at again after it goes live, on a schedule and whenever something material changes.

**Why it matters.** A system that was fair and accurate at purchase can stop being so as the people, the data or the model change. Nobody inside notices. The people on the receiving end do: they are scored against a world that no longer exists.

**The UK position.** UK GDPR Article 35 requires a controller to review processing against its impact assessment at least when the risk changes. The Information Commission (ICO), in AI guidance now under review, says: "After deployment, you should implement monitoring, the frequency of which should be proportional to the impact an incorrect output may have on individuals." It also expects documented thresholds for when a model must be retrained. For banks with internal model approval, the Prudential Regulation Authority expects ongoing performance monitoring, including of supplier models, at a frequency set by each model's tier. The EU AI Act describes risk management for high-risk systems as a continuous process across the whole lifecycle and requires deployers to monitor operation.

**What good looks like.** Each system has a review date and named triggers: a model or supplier change, a new use, an incident, a change in the law. Thresholds are written down. Reviews are minuted and feed treatment decisions and board reports.

**Reading the answer.** Ask when the most consequential system last changed and whether anyone reassessed it. A review that rereads the document and not the system does not count.
