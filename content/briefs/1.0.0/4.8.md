# External reporting honesty

**What it measures.** Whether public statements about the organisation's AI (marketing, bids, investor material, the annual report) are checked against what the systems really do before publication, and who signs that off.

**Why it matters.** An overstated AI claim is an untruth told to people who cannot check it. Customers pay for a capability that is not there, investors price the company on it, and people are told a human reviews decisions when none does. The harm is to their ability to choose on accurate information.

**The UK position.** No rule is specific to AI; the general law on misleading statements applies. For consumers, unfair commercial practices are prohibited, including an overall presentation likely to deceive even where each statement is true. Since 6 April 2025 the Competition and Markets Authority can itself fine up to £300,000 or, if higher, 10% of turnover. Misleading advertising to business buyers is a separate criminal offence. The advertising regulator's advice on AI products: "Hold evidence to back up any objective claims about what your product can do". Issuers of traded securities must compensate investors for published statements a senior manager knew were misleading, or was reckless about.

**What good looks like.** Each claim traced to evidence before release, a named sign-off, re-checks as systems change, published corrections, and independent assurance at the top level.

**Reading the answer.** Claims about governance (audited, human-reviewed, compliant) are the easiest to disprove from the organisation's own records. A claim true at publication can become false after a system change.

**A defensible figure.** The Advertising Standards Authority identified around 16,000 unique online adverts using AI as a marketing term between July and September 2024; 68% were for business-to-business products.
