# Rights readiness

**What it measures.** Whether the organisation can find, correct, delete or stop using a person's data when they ask, including data in AI training sets, outputs, profiles and suppliers' systems, within the legal time limit.

**Why it matters.** These rights are how a person checks what is held about them, corrects it and withdraws from uses they did not choose. If AI puts their data beyond the organisation's reach, the right exists on paper only: the person stays profiled or wrongly described.

**The UK position.** The UK GDPR gives rights of access, rectification, erasure and objection, with limits but no exception for AI. Since the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025, access covers what a reasonable and proportionate search can find. Since 19 June 2026 controllers must also handle individuals' complaints (Data Protection Act 2018, section 164A). Guidance from the Information Commission (ICO), under review, says: "When procuring an AI service, you must choose one which allows individual rights to be protected and enabled, in order to meet your obligations as a controller." The regulator has said output filters may not be enough: they leave data in the model.

**What good looks like.** Rights procedures name each AI system and where its personal data sits; supplier contracts require help with requests; deletion and suppression routes are tested; turnaround and completeness are measured.

**Reading the answer.** The usual gap is a sound general process never tried against an AI system. Ask for the last request that touched one.

**A defensible figure.** An ICO enforcement notice against Bristol City Council (27 August 2025) records 189 overdue access requests in February 2024, up from 170, which the council said would take 50 months to clear (paragraphs 13-15). It is one council and not about AI.
