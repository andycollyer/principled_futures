# 1.8 Documentation and records (researcher log, 2 October 2026)

Method: content/research/METHOD.md, including "Lessons from Domain 3" and "Citations for the source directory".
Every factual claim in the existing briefing (src/lib/articles.ts, id 1.8) was listed and checked against the
primary source text. Every web source below was fetched with curl and read as raw page text with the tags
stripped; PDFs were read as text extracted from the PDF itself (pypdf). No summarising tool was used. The
quotation, the figure and all ten reading-list quotes were matched as exact strings using the project's own
checker functions (scripts/source_check.py `fetch` and `norm`): all ten passed.

Angle. The records and accountability brief (5.8) covers the data protection duty to demonstrate compliance and
the regulator's powers to demand records. This brief takes the board's view: company law on minutes, directors'
duties and relief, the litigation duty to preserve documents, and what a per-system record should hold. No source,
quotation or figure is shared with 5.8. The draft brief for criterion 2.4 uses the EU six-month log period as its
figure, so this brief does not state it and the reading-list quote for Article 26 avoids those words. Briefs 1.1
and 7.4 cite section 248(1); this brief's figure is from section 248(2), which neither uses.

- s.248(1): "Every company must cause minutes of all proceedings at meetings of its directors to be recorded."
- s.249(1): minutes "if purporting to be authenticated by the chairman of the meeting or by the chairman of the next
  directors' meeting, are evidence (in Scotland, sufficient evidence) of the proceedings at the meeting".
- s.172(1): "have regard (amongst other matters) to" (b) employees, (c) suppliers, customers and others, (d) the
  community and the environment.
- s.414CZA(1)-(2): strategic report "must include a statement ... which describes how the directors have had regard
  to the matters set out in section 172(1)(a) to (f)"; not where the company qualifies as medium-sized.
- s.1157(1): "he acted honestly and reasonably, and ... he ought fairly to be excused, the court may relieve him,
  either wholly or in part".
- PD57AD 3.1(1): a person who knows it is or may become a party to proceedings must "take reasonable steps to
  preserve documents in its control that may be relevant to any issue in the proceedings"; 4.1: including documents
  "which might otherwise be deleted or destroyed in accordance with a document retention policy"; 13.1: production
  "in a manner which preserves metadata".

## Not verified, and cautions

- No case law was read. "No statute gives a safe harbour for good paperwork" rests on the text of sections 172 and
  1157 only. How courts weigh minutes when deciding section 172 or section 1157 questions was not researched.
- Practice Direction 57AD applies in the Business and Property Courts of England and Wales; the brief says so.
  Scotland and Northern Ireland procedure was not read. The page says it was updated 29 September 2022; later
  amendments, if any, would show there.
- "large companies must report": the section exempts companies that qualify as medium-sized; small companies do not
  prepare a strategic report (not read in this pass, sections 414A-414B).
- Section 248 says nothing about what a minute must contain. The brief does not claim that AI decisions must be
  minuted in any particular form.
- The quotation's "It" refers to the term "document" (para 2.6, first sentence). The brief introduces it that way.
- The criterion's top level mentions section 172 considerations "minuted throughout". The law does not require
  that; the brief presents records as evidence, not as a statutory duty beyond section 248.
- The algorithmic transparency standard is mandatory by government policy, not statute, and for central government
  only. It was cut from the brief for length and stays in the reading list.
- Body word count: 279. Reading list: 10 items.
