## Not verified, and cautions

- No case law was read. "No statute gives a safe harbour for good paperwork" rests on the text of sections 172 and
  1157 only. How courts weigh minutes when deciding section 172 or section 1157 questions was not researched.
- Practice Direction 57AD applies in the Business and Property Courts of England and Wales; the brief says so.
  Scotland and Northern Ireland procedure was not read. The page says it was updated 29 September 2022; later
  amendments, if any, would show there.
- "large companies must report": the section exempts companies that qualify as medium-sized; small companies do not
  prepare a strategic report (not read in this pass, sections 414A-414B).
- Section 248 says nothing about what a minute must contain. The brief does not claim that AI decisions must be
  minuted in any particular form.
- The quotation's "It" refers to the term "document" (para 2.6, first sentence). The brief introduces it that way.
- The criterion's top level mentions section 172 considerations "minuted throughout". The law does not require
  that; the brief presents records as evidence, not as a statutory duty beyond section 248.
- The algorithmic transparency standard is mandatory by government policy, not statute, and for central government
  only. It was cut from the brief for length and stays in the reading list.
