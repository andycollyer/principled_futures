# 8.7 Sustainability governance (researcher log, 2 October 2026)

Method: content/research/METHOD.md, including "Lessons from Domain 3" and "Citations for the source directory".
Every factual claim in the existing briefing (src/lib/articles.ts, id 8.7) was listed and checked against the
primary source text. Every web source below was fetched with curl and read as raw page text with the tags
stripped; PDFs were read as text extracted from the PDF itself (pypdf). No summarising tool was used for any
quotation or figure. The quotation, the figures and all ten reading-list quotes were matched as exact strings in
that raw text (after normalising white space and quote marks).

Angle. The legacy briefing rests the whole case on one line of the European voluntary standard. This brief puts the
UK position first: the director's duty that applies to every company, the disclosure duty that applies to large
ones, and the one procurement rule that asks any bidder for board sign-off.

Other sentences in the brief that rest on a specific passage (paraphrased, not quoted):
- "must have regard to the impact of the company's operations on the community and the environment": s.172(1)(d).
- "the courts leave the weighing to directors acting in good faith": ClientEarth v Shell [2023] EWHC 1897 (Ch),
  para. 28: "it is for directors themselves to determine (acting in good faith) how best to promote the success of
  a company for the benefit of its members as a whole". One first-instance decision; "the courts" is a
  generalisation the lead may wish to soften to "the High Court has left".
- "must describe their governance of climate-related risks": s.414CB(2A)(a).
- "approved by the board and signed by a director": PPN 006 technical standard paras 24 and 25: "Your Carbon
  Reduction Plan should clearly state that board of directors (or equivalent management body) approval has been
  given with the date of approval" and "must be signed off by a director (or equivalent) or designated member".
  Note that para. 24 is a "should" and para. 25 a "must"; the brief says "needs".
- "only half presented all the required information on targets and progress against them": the bullet reads
  "Disclosures in relation to climate-related targets, and the assessment of progress against these targets using
  key performance indicators (KPIs), require improvement. Only half of the companies presented all of this
  information."

## Not verified, and cautions

- The quotation and figure are about climate governance in 20 large companies, not about sustainability generally
  and not about SMEs. The brief says "Reviewing first-year climate disclosures".
- "the courts leave the weighing to directors" rests on one High Court permission decision. Whether it was appealed
  was not checked.
- Thresholds (500 employees, £500 million) are as shown on legislation.gov.uk today. The page carries the standard
  notice that revised legislation "may not be fully up to date". Limited liability partnerships have parallel rules
  that were not read.
- The UK position sentence lists "traded, banks, insurers, on AIM or ... turnover above £500 million"; the statute
  says "traded company", "banking company", "authorised insurance company", "a company carrying on insurance market
  activity", AIM, and "high turnover company". The brief compresses these.
- UK SRS S2 is in the reading list but not named in the brief, for length. Who must apply it, and from when, was
  not researched; the document read does not say.
- The Corporate Governance Code's application date was not used (a date from that document is already an anchor
  for another brief).
- The criterion's top level ("Integrated into strategy and remuneration; externally credible") is supported only as
  a disclosure item for larger reporters. No source read shows that pay linkage improves outcomes.
- "including the energy its AI uses" in "Why it matters" is the thread to the AI footprint brief, not a sourced
  finding.
- Body word count: 286 words including the six bold labels. Reading list: 10 items.
