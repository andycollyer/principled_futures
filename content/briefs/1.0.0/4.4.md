# Contestability

**What it measures.** Whether a person affected by a significant automated decision can put their side, have a person look at it again and challenge the outcome, by a route that works in practice.

**Why it matters.** Even an accurate system gets individual cases wrong, and the person affected may be the only one placed to see the error. Without a working route, someone refused credit, a job or a service carries a mistake they cannot correct, with nobody answerable to them.

**The UK position.** Since February 2026 the UK GDPR requires safeguards wherever a significant decision is taken solely by automated processing of personal data: information about the decision, and the means to make representations, obtain human intervention and contest it. The Information Commissioner's Office's draft guidance, still a consultation draft, says of human intervention: "You must ensure this involves a review of the decision, with the possibility that you could change it." The reviewer needs discretion and authority to alter the decision. Spot checks do not count, because the review must be one the person can start.

**What good looks like.** People are told how to challenge when the decision is given; one simple route covers all three steps; the reviewer sees the original data and whatever the person adds; each review is recorded; outcomes are analysed and fed back into the system.

**Reading the answer.** A general complaints line that cannot reopen the decision is the usual gap. Frequent reversals may point to a fault in the system, not in the route.

**A defensible figure.** The organisation must act on such a request within one month, extendable by two further months for complex or numerous requests (UK GDPR Article 12A).
