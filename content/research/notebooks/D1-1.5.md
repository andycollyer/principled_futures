# 1.5 Third-party and procurement governance (researcher log, 2 October 2026)

Method: content/research/METHOD.md, including "Lessons from Domain 3" and "Citations for the source directory".
Every factual claim in the existing briefing (src/lib/articles.ts, id 1.5) was listed and checked against the
primary source text. Every web source below was fetched with curl and read as raw page text with the tags
stripped; PDFs were read as text extracted from the PDF itself (pypdf). No summarising tool was used. The
quotation, the figures and all ten reading-list quotes were then matched as exact strings using the project's own
checker functions (scripts/source_check.py `fetch` and `norm`): all ten passed.

Angle. The vendor fairness brief (3.7) covers checking a supplier's fairness claim under equality and data
protection law, using the ICO recruitment audit. This brief takes the wider governance view: who stays accountable,
what is checked before and during use, what goes in the contract, and how to leave. Different evidence: the PRA
outsourcing statement, the Procurement Act, NCSC, the AI security code and the Bank of England/FCA survey. Two
documents on the 3.7 reading list are also here (Guidelines for AI procurement) or were read and left out (UK GDPR
Article 28); no quotation or figure is shared.

## Not verified, and cautions

- The quotation is addressed to PRA-regulated firms. The brief says so ("tells banks and insurers").
- SS2/21 treats buying an off-the-shelf model as a third party arrangement that is not outsourcing. The brief's
  "for material outsourcing" wording keeps the audit and exit expectations where the statement puts them.
- "Elsewhere, beyond data protection duties, the expectations are guidance" is my summary. Sector rules outside
  financial services and public procurement (for example medical devices) were not researched.
- Procurement Act s.52 has exceptions (s.52(2), (6)); the threshold can be changed by regulations (s.52(5)). Whether
  it has been changed was checked only through the annotations on the section: none shown.
- The ICO toolkit is under review and prints no date.
- The survey figure is financial services only and counts use cases, not firms. The brief's second sentence compares
  with the 2022 all-area share (17%); the 2022 business-area split was not read.
- The liability statement is from a document of the previous government. No later government statement was found.
- No UK court decision on liability for a bought AI tool was read.
- Body word count: 287. Reading list: 10 items.
