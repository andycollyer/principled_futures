# Proportionality and documentation

**What it measures.** Whether the depth of risk work matches the organisation's size and the stakes of each system, and whether there is a record to show for it.

**Why it matters.** With too little, people harmed by a system find that nobody can show what was considered or why. With too much, a small firm spends its effort on paperwork for trivial tools.

**The UK position.** UK GDPR Article 24 scales the duty: measures must reflect the nature, scope, context and purposes of processing and its risks, and the controller must be able to demonstrate compliance. The Information Commission (ICO), in guidance under review, says: "Your governance and risk management capabilities need to be proportionate to your use of AI." Its audit framework says no single approach fits all. Government policy since 2023 is that burdens should not fall disproportionately on smaller companies. In the EU, the AI Act as amended in 2026 lets small and medium-sized enterprises and small mid-caps give technical documentation in a simplified form. The Commission's form was not found published. Simplification changes the format, not the level of protection.

**What good looks like.** One file per system, as deep as the stakes: what it is, how it was classed and why, the treatment decisions, the controls, incidents and reviews. It is kept current and can be produced on request.

**Reading the answer.** Ask for the file on the highest-stakes system. Lean is fine. Empty or stale is not.

**A defensible figure.** The European Commission estimated that extending small-company relief to small mid-caps would ease implementation for a further 8,250 companies. This was its estimate when the change was proposed.
