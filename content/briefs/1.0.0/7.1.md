# AI literacy

**What it measures.** Whether the people who use AI for the organisation, employees and contractors alike, know enough to use it safely, and whether records show it.

**Why it matters.** Untrained staff paste confidential or personal information into public tools, accept wrong output and pass it on. Customers and the public are affected by work nobody checked; staff are blamed for errors they were never equipped to catch.

**The UK position.** No UK statute sets a general AI literacy duty. Organisations within the scope of the EU AI Act have, since February 2025, had to take measures to support the AI literacy of their staff and of others who operate or use AI on their behalf. The 2026 amendment added: "This obligation does not require providers or deployers to guarantee any specific level of AI literacy of any individual." National authorities have been able to enforce it since August 2026. The European Commission says there is no duty to measure employees' knowledge, but that relying on the instructions for use is not enough. Acas advises that staff may need training and that AI output should be checked for accuracy, tone and bias.

**What good looks like.** Training matched to role and tool, covering contractors; records of who was trained, on what and when; content refreshed when tools change; competence tested, not attendance counted.

**Reading the answer.** One recorded webinar, with no list of who watched, is the weak answer. So is a programme that stops at employees.

**A defensible figure.** In a government-commissioned survey of 801 UK employers, carried out in 2024 and published in January 2026, 11% said staff had undertaken AI training in the previous 12 months.
